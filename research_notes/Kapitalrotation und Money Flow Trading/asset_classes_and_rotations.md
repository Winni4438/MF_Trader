## 1. A practical classification of tradable assets by how they behave in flows

### Takeaway
In practice assets fall into four flow buckets:
- **Risk/growth assets:** equities (high-beta tech, small caps, cyclicals, EM), HY credit, carry and commodity FX, crypto, industrial commodities.
- **Safe havens:** core government bonds, USD, JPY, CHF, gold.
- **Inflation hedges:** energy and commodities, TIPS, gold, value equities.
- **Liquidity/cash:** T-bills, money-market funds, USD.

Which bucket an asset sits in depends on the regime. In inflation and rate shocks, long Treasuries behave like risk assets (2022, 2026). In liquidity crunches, gold and even Treasuries are sold for cash (March 2020, March 2026). In a US-centred policy shock, the USD and long Treasuries fall together (April 2025). A money-flow system therefore needs a regime layer as well as a static map.

### Cited Findings
- **Safe-haven FX: CHF and JPY.** The Swiss franc and the yen tend to appreciate against the USD when US stock prices fall. The effect shows up over horizons from **a few hours to several days**, is stronger in crises, and was especially strong for the yen in the GFC. CHF, EUR, JPY and GBP also rose against the USD when US bond prices rose and FX volatility increased. The CEPR version ties these moves to carry-trade losses. — [Ranaldo & Söderlind, SNB WP 2007-17 / Review of Finance 2010](https://www.snb.ch/en/mmr/papers/id/working_paper_2007_17); [CEPR DP7249](https://cepr.org/publications/DP7249)
- **Robeco, S&P 500 drawdowns since the 1970s:**
  - USD and JPY performed well during equity drawdowns. The euro has historically been a weaker haven but outperformed in the 2025 tariff drawdown. The franc is named as a good alternative haven.
  - Highly rated corporate bonds are not reliable havens: liquidity dries up and they carry credit risk. Inflation-linked bonds become less liquid in crises.
  - Commodities tended to do well in crashes.
  - Defensive equities beat the broad market by **>10% on average** in corrections of more than 15% over the past 60 years.
  - [Robeco, Oct 2025](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)
- **WisdomTree (EUR-based, monthly, July 2000–Dec 2019):** gold, cash and government bonds delivered strong positive performance in all equity-drawdown periods. The JPY did well in most of them. Commodities and high-yield bonds behaved cyclically. Gold's volatility is equity-like, but its drawdown profile is not. — [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/fr/insights/blog/defensive-assets-is-playing-too-safe-too-risky/pdf)
- **Carry FX (investment currencies vs. funding currencies):**
  - Exchange rates between high- and low-yield currencies are negatively skewed (crash risk) because carry trades unwind suddenly.
  - A rise in the VIX coincides with speculators reducing carry positions and with carry losses.
  - Currencies with similar interest rates co-move.
  - [Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen, NBER w14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473); VIX-specific wording in the [preliminary draft](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/M-Brunnermeier.pdf)
- **USD in a liquidity crunch.** In the week and a half after the WHO pandemic declaration (11 Mar 2020), the dollar rose **7%** against currencies of central banks with standing Fed swap lines. It rose **12%** against currencies of countries later given temporary swap lines. Average FX-swap spreads widened from ~20–35 bp in January to **~150 bp** in mid-March 2020. — [Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2024/0521); [Richmond Fed Econ Focus](https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/publications/research/econ_focus/2024/q4/federal_reserve.pdf)
- **USD and Treasuries normally.** In risk-off the USD usually appreciates and Treasury yields usually fall. The yield and the dollar are normally positively correlated, as they were through much of 2024. That correlation turned negative after 2 April 2025 and also broke down in the early-2020 dash for cash. — [ECB FSR Nov 2025 special feature](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/special/html/ecb.fsrart202511_01~fdf147a04a.en.html)
- **Bitcoin is a risk asset, not a haven (IMF, 2022):**
  - The correlation of daily BTC and S&P 500 returns was **0.01** in 2017–2019 and **0.36** in 2020–2021.
  - The correlation with MSCI EM equities was **0.34** in 2020–2021, a 17-fold rise.
  - BTC volatility explained about one-sixth of S&P 500 volatility during the pandemic.
  - [IMF via Business Insider](https://businessinsider.nl/bitcoin-and-stocks-are-increasingly-moving-together-ramping-up-contagion-risks-across-markets-says-imf); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/imf-warns-of-contagion-risks-to-stock-and-bitcoin-markets/)
- **Long government bonds depend on the inflation regime:**
  - Invesco (data since 1952): the stock-bond correlation was **positive 98% of the time** when five-year annualised inflation exceeded **3%**. — [Trustnet](https://www.trustnet.com/news/13441497/higher-inflation-means-bonds-no-longer-protect-investors-when-stocks-fall)
  - Vanguard's model puts the threshold for positive correlation at a 10-year average inflation near **3.5%**. — [Vanguard](https://nl.vanguard/professional/insights/portfolio-construction/can-investors-count-on-stock-bond-diversification)
  - Swinkels et al.: correlation is positive when inflation is ≥3% and rates rise together, mainly in the US. — [InvestmentNews NZ](https://investmentnews.co.nz/investment-news/changing-correlations-high-inflation-rates-regimes-keep-bonds-and-shares-in-lock-step)
- **Short-dated vs. long-dated Treasuries.** From late March to mid-April 2025, the correlation of 10Y and 30Y Treasuries with equities turned **positive**, while the **2Y** note's correlation stayed **negative**, so it still worked as a haven. — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)
- **Gold.** Gold is a hedge against stocks on average and a safe haven in extreme stock-market conditions, but the haven property is **short-lived**. The 2006 working paper puts it at about **15 trading days after an extreme shock**. Gold is **not** a safe haven for bonds. — [Baur & Lucey IIIS DP198](https://www.tcd.ie/triss/assets/PDFs/iiis/iiisdp198.pdf); [published version abstract](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/14038)
- **Funding currencies beyond JPY.** In the August 2024 unwind, offshore renminbi (CNH) and the Malaysian ringgit, which are not normally funding currencies, also appreciated notably. BIS put the size of the carry trade going into the event at roughly **¥40 trillion (~$250bn)**, probably an underestimate. — [BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.pdf); secondary summaries [thefullfx](https://thefullfx.com/?p=5176), [globaltrading.net](https://globaltrading.net/?p=36090)
- **Equity sub-class behaviour in a 2026 AI-disruption risk-off (Feb 2026):**
  - Week of 2–6 Feb: money rotated out of big tech and software into equal-weight, mid-cap and small-cap indexes, which rose 2–4%.
  - Week of 9–12 Feb: **defensives** led (Consumer Staples, Real Estate, Utilities), Materials rose, Financials were worst (**−4%**), semiconductors were a relative bright spot, and small caps lagged large caps by ~1%.
  - [ICE Market Storylines, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.ice.com/insights/conversations/inside-the-ice-house/market-storylines/2-13-2026)
- **Cash and T-bills are the only asset that held up in the March 2020 Treasury sell-off.** Treasury prices fell and yields rose, against the usual stress pattern. Bid-ask spreads blew out first in off-the-run and then in on-the-run Treasuries. Sellers included foreign official and private investors, hedge funds and other levered investors. — [BIS speech, Oct 2020](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20201019-what-happened-what-have-we-learned-it-lessons-covid-19-stress-financial-system)
- **Volatility as an asset.** On 5 Feb 2018 the VIX jumped a record **+20.01 points, from 17.31 to 37.32**. Short-volatility ETPs were destroyed: the XIV note went from $1.9bn to $63m in assets in one session. — [Macroption](https://macroption.com/vix-all-time-high); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-06/the-day-the-vix-doubled-tales-of-volmageddon)

## 2. Catalogue of common rotation types: drivers, sequence, duration, frequency, leaders and laggards

### Takeaway
The recurring rotations are:
1. **Risk-on/risk-off.** Equities, crypto, HY and carry FX move against Treasuries, JPY, CHF, gold and USD.
2. **Liquidity "dash for cash".** Everything except the USD and T-bills is sold.
3. **Inflation/rate shock.** Stocks and long bonds fall together; USD and energy rise; gold falls when real yields rise.
4. **US-policy-credibility shock.** USD and long Treasuries fall together; CHF, EUR, gold, Bunds and 2Y Treasuries rise.
5. **Carry unwind.** JPY and CHF rally; Nikkei, MXN, crypto and momentum stocks crash.
6. **Intra-equity rotations:** growth↔value, mega-cap↔equal-weight/small, US↔Europe/EM.
7. **Crypto-internal rotation:** BTC → ETH → altcoins in up-legs; the reverse, amplified, in down-legs.
8. **Calendar and event flows:** month- and quarter-end rebalancing, pre-FOMC drift, CPI/NFP/PMI repricings.

The fast shocks (types 1, 4, 5 and event rotations) usually play out in **hours to a few days**, often ending in a policy reversal. The regime rotations (inflation, USD cycle, growth/value) take **months**.

### Cited Findings
**Risk-on/risk-off (growth scare)**
- In Q4 2018, the S&P 500 fell ~19.8% peak to trough (20 Sep–24 Dec). The 10Y yield fell from ~3.24% in November to 2.69% at year-end. Long-dated Treasuries returned **+4.6%** and gold rose **~7–7.5%** in the quarter. — [Pathstone](https://pathstone.com/quarterly-flash-report-december-2018); [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/fixed-income-content-hub/exposure-to-us-treasury-bonds-and-gold/); [WE Family Offices](https://www.wefamilyoffices.com/q4-2018-recap/)
- In Feb 2026 risk-off, Treasuries rallied: the 2Y fell ~3 bp and the 10Y and 30Y ~10 bp each in a week. Financials were down 4%, Bitcoin dipped to ~$60k, and the DXY retraced half of its 96→97 move. — [ICE, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.ice.com/insights/conversations/inside-the-ice-house/market-storylines/2-13-2026)

**Dash for cash (liquidity crunch)**
- In March 2020, Treasuries sold off alongside stocks; dealers were unable or unwilling to absorb the selling. — [BIS speech](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20201019-what-happened-what-have-we-learned-it-lessons-covid-19-stress-financial-system)
- The USD rose 7–12% in about 1.5 weeks. — [Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2024/0521)
- Gold fell ~10% from 24 Feb to 16 Mar 2020 as investors sold it for liquidity, margin calls and redemptions. AJ Bell says the same pattern occurred in 2008. — [ii.co.uk](https://ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/why-did-safe-haven-gold-fall-value-during-market-sell-ii512586)
- Bitcoin fell ~50% over 12–13 March 2020. — [BlockTower](https://blocktower.substack.com/p/towerwatch-10-the-great-sell-off); [Daily Maverick/Bloomberg](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2020-03-13-gold-bursts-above-1700-with-market-mayhem-stoking-haven-demand-2/)

**Inflation/rate-shock rotation (stocks and bonds down together)**
- 2022 calendar year:
  - S&P 500 **−18.1%**; the 10Y yield started the year at 1.51%; the 10-year Treasury **−17.8%**. — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/01/26/2022-was-the-worst-year-since-1937-for-this-invest)
  - US Aggregate **−13%**. — [UCU blog](https://newpathway.ca/?p=89106)
  - 60/40 portfolio **−14%**. — [Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/ca-en/insights/market-review-2022-after-a-down-year-looking-to-the-past-as-a-guide)
  - Nasdaq **−33%**, BTC **−64%**. — [TradingView recap](https://it.tradingview.com/chart/SPX/wOPLfuju-Performance-recap-2022)
  - Gold roughly flat (−0.27% to −0.62%). — [Xetra-Gold](https://www.xetra-gold.com/en/detail/gold-war-eines-der-erfolgreichsten-assets-2022/)
  - DXY 106.59 in July 2022, the highest since Dec 2002. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/markets/bitcoin-faces-fresh-pressure-as-us-dollar-crushes-gold-risk-assets)
  - Robeco gives the 2022 return on 10Y Treasuries as −16.7%, slightly different from the Fool's −17.8%, probably a different measure. — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)
- **2026 version: oil shock, positive stock-bond correlation, gold down.**
  - 23 Sep 2026: the 10Y rose as high as **5.13%** (highest since 2007), the sharpest one-day rise since 9 Apr 2025. Brent was up 3.8% to **$103.08**, the Nasdaq −1.13%, the S&P −0.75%. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/energy/treasury-yields-oil-stocks-rcna599398)
  - Same day: gold futures −1.33% to $4,318 (−7.26% over the month), DXY +0.4% to 100.56, BTC −2.3% to $84,256. — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/gold-selloff-shows-why-5-treasury-yields-are-the-bigger-threat-200688251)

**US-policy-credibility shock (April 2025)**
- The 10Y yield rose almost **50 bp** between 4 and 11 April 2025, the third-largest weekly increase since 1986. The dollar fell ~12% against the euro over 2025 to November, of which ~7pp came after 1 April. Bund yields fell over the same period. — [ECB FSR](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/special/html/ecb.fsrart202511_01~fdf147a04a.en.html)
- On days when long Treasuries co-moved positively with stocks, gold co-moved negatively, suggesting gold was used in place of long Treasuries as the hedge. TIC data show **$47bn** of outflows from long-term Treasuries in April 2025. — [NBER Digest on Acharya & Laarits](https://www.nber.org/index%2ephp/digest/us-treasury-bonds-and-trade-policy-uncertainty)

**Carry-trade unwind (JPY)**
- Sequence: BoJ hike on 31 July 2024, then weak US data and a dollar sell-off, then yen surge, forced unwinds, and the peak on 5 August. Markets recovered within the week. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/etf-strategist-content-hub/notes-desk-signal-through-noise/); [BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.pdf)
- Volmex argues that US growth concerns, rather than the BoJ alone, were the main catalyst. — [Volmex](https://volmex.finance/Volmex%20Research%20Series_%20Crash%20of%20August%205,%202024.pdf)
- The yen strengthened ~6% within days. — [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/ch/fr/insights/blog/positioning-for-a-stronger-yen)

**Growth↔value, mega-cap↔broad market, AI-theme rotations**
- 2–6 Feb 2026: the IGV software ETF fell ~10% on fears of AI displacement after a new Anthropic product release. Money rotated into equal-weight, mid-cap and small-cap stocks, and the Dow broke 50,000 for the first time while the S&P was flat. Contagion spread to private-credit names with software-loan exposure, brokers and wealth managers, data providers, and trucking/logistics. — [ICE](https://www.ice.com/insights/conversations/inside-the-ice-house/market-storylines/2-13-2026)
- 8 Oct 2026: AI/semiconductor rotation. After an FT report that OpenAI's annualised revenue was $20bn below earlier signals: SOXX −3.5%, the DRAM memory ETF −5%, Nvidia nearly −3%, Nasdaq −1.3%, S&P −0.5%, Dow +0.1%. PepsiCo (+3.5%) and Chipotle (+6%, on a report that Starbucks explored buying it) rose. — [Yahoo Finance, 8 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/markets-news-oct-8-2026-105400965.html)

**US↔Europe / USD cycle**
- The dollar fell ~12% against the euro in 2025 to November. Speculative USD positioning flipped from multi-year net long to net short in April 2025 and stayed short for months. Euro-area flows into US assets fell sharply in April and rebounded in May. — [ECB FSR](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/financial-stability-publications/fsr/special/html/ecb.fsrart202511_01~fdf147a04a.en.html)
- The euro outperformed as a haven in the 2025 drawdown. — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)
- In 2026 the DXY rebounded, from ~96–97 in February ([ICE](https://www.ice.com/insights/conversations/inside-the-ice-house/market-storylines/2-13-2026)) to 100.56 on 23 Sep ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/gold-selloff-shows-why-5-treasury-yields-are-the-bigger-threat-200688251)) and 102.13 on 8 Oct ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/markets-news-oct-8-2026-105400965.html)).

**Equities↔bonds rebalancing (month- and quarter-end)**
- Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone (2025), "The Unintended Consequences of Rebalancing":
  - Calendar and threshold rebalancing by large funds has a market-wide impact and creates predictable price patterns.
  - When equities are overweight, the resulting selling lowers **next-day equity returns by ~17 bp**, after controlling for momentum, reversals and macro information.
  - The estimated annual cost is ~$16bn, and front-runners can profit from it.
  - [Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/what-do-pensions-lose-from-rebalancing); [CFA Institute blog](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2025/rebalancings-hidden-cost-how-predictable-trades-cost-pension-funds-billions)

**Crypto-internal rotation**
- Practitioner research says altseasons historically began when Bitcoin dominance hit a local peak, with capital rotating from BTC into ETH and then into smaller altcoins. In 2021, dominance fell from ~70% to ~40% between February and May, and altcoins then fell harder than BTC in the May sell-off. ETH/BTC is treated as the earliest signal and the Altcoin Season Index as lagging. In September 2025 the index briefly crossed 75, but the rally faded. — [Block Scholes/Bybit](https://www.blockscholes.com/research/bybit-x-block-scholes-quarterly-report-altcoin-season-and-the-evolving-role-of-bitcoin); search summary of practitioner pieces
- In the 10 Oct 2025 cascade, thin altcoin liquidity and Binance's oracle pricing amplified liquidations. — [Coin360](https://coin360.com/news/crypto-market-liquidation-2025-trump-tariffs); [RockawayX](https://rockawayx.com/insights/crypto-market-update-october-2025)

**Event rotations (FOMC/CPI/NFP/PMI)**
- **Pre-FOMC drift** (Lucca & Moench): since 1994, large S&P 500 excess returns in the 24h before scheduled FOMC announcements, about **33 bp vs ~1 bp** on other days. More than 80% of the US equity premium was earned in that window. Nothing comparable was found in Treasuries or the USD. — [NY Fed Liberty Street](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2012/07/the-puzzling-pre-fomc-announcement-drift)
- **NFP shock, 5 Jun 2026:**
  - Payrolls +172k beat expectations, December hike odds rose from 48% to 63%, and BTC briefly fell below $60k. — [Schwab Network](https://schwabnetwork.com/video/friday-s-final-takeaways-labor-data-bitcoin); [Investrade](https://investrade.com/mid-morning-look-june-05-2026/)
  - Per a CNN search snippet (page blocked to fetch): S&P −2.64% (worst day since October), 10Y to 4.54%, gold −3.5%; BTC weakness was linked to Strategy disclosing its first BTC sale since 2022. — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/05/markets/stock-market-sell-off-fed)
- **PMI shock, 23 Sep 2026:** the 10Y jumped 9.4 bp to 5.042% within minutes of the S&P Global PMI release. — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/gold-selloff-shows-why-5-treasury-yields-are-the-bigger-threat-200688251)

### Inferences
**Rotation catalogue** (synthesis; durations and frequencies come from the cases in sections 3–4):

| Rotation | Typical trigger | Leaders (move first / most) | Laggards / destination | Typical duration of impulse | Observed frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk-off, growth scare | weak macro data, earnings or AI-capex doubts, bank stress | Nasdaq/semis, small caps, crypto, HY, AUD/MXN; destination 2Y/10Y UST, JPY, CHF, gold | defensives, staples, utilities outperform | 1–5 days for the impulse; weeks if a correction | several per year (5% dips ~2×/year) |
| Dash for cash | funding or margin stress, pandemic-type shock | USD (DXY, USD vs EM), T-bills; everything else sold including gold and off-the-run UST | gold and long UST recover after central bank backstops | ~1–2 weeks (Mar 2020: 9–20 Mar) | rare (2008, 2020; partial Mar 2026 in gold) |
| Inflation/rate shock | hot CPI/NFP/PMI, oil shock, hawkish central bank | long UST yields up, USD up, energy up; Nasdaq, long-duration growth, gold, BTC down | value/energy outperform | single days (data releases) inside multi-month regimes | 2022; 2026 recurring |
| US-policy-credibility shock | tariffs, Fed-independence fears, fiscal | USD down, 10Y/30Y UST down, US equities down; CHF, EUR, gold, Bunds, 2Y UST up | non-US equities relative ↑ | 3–7 days, then policy reversal | 2025 (April), echoes in 2026 |
| Carry unwind | BoJ hawkish surprise, VIX spike, US growth scare | JPY (also CHF, CNH) up; Nikkei/TOPIX, MXN, crypto, momentum/AI stocks down | rebound within a week (Aug 2024) | 2–5 trading days | 2007/08, 2015/16, Aug 2024 |
| Crypto leverage cascade | macro headline on a weekend or illiquid hours, funding/OI extremes | alts > ETH > BTC down; stablecoins as destination | TradFi spillover limited | hours (minutes to bottom), days to stabilise | several per year at smaller size |
| Mega-cap → broad/equal-weight/small | AI-disruption or valuation fears, rate cuts | equal-weight, mid/small, value, Dow outperform Nasdaq 100 | — | days to weeks | multiple per year in 2024–2026 |
| Month/quarter-end rebalancing | drift of equity/bond weights | sell the winner, buy the loser around month-end | next-day reversal ~17 bp | 1–2 days | monthly/quarterly |
| Pre-FOMC / data-day | scheduled events | equities (pre-FOMC), front-end rates (NFP/CPI) | — | 24h window | 8 FOMC/yr, 12 CPI/NFP/yr |

- **Lead-lag pattern seen repeatedly:** crypto and Asian equities (weekend or overnight) often move first, before US cash-equity hours. Examples: the Oct 2025 cascade on Friday night into the weekend, 5 Aug 2024 with Japan opening the stress day, and the Iran strikes over the weekend of 28 Feb–1 Mar 2026. This is a design hint for a 24h detection system, not a tested statistic.

### Gaps
- No rigorous academic test of crypto lead-lag (BTC→ETH→alts) was found; dominance and altcoin-index thresholds are practitioner conventions.
- No source quantified month-end flow sizes for 2024–2026 or whether the pre-FOMC drift has decayed since the 2015 publication.
- No quantified source for growth↔value or US↔EM rotation frequencies and payoffs.

## 3. How often risk-off episodes occur, how long they last, and how far safe havens move

### Takeaway
S&P 500 declines of ≥5% happen about **twice a year**, ≥10% roughly **every 1.5–2.5 years**, and ≥20% about **every 6–8 years**. Extreme VIX spikes (≥45) have happened about 16 times since 1987. Recent spikes have normalised in **days to weeks**: the VIX went from above 30 to below 20 in about 8 trading days in the latest episode measured, and the 2025 normalisation was the fastest on record. Documented haven moves:
- 2Y yields: up to ~−97 bp in 3 days (SVB 2023, the extreme case).
- 10Y yields: about −10 bp in a one-week growth scare (Feb 2026, Oct 2025), and −55 bp over Q4 2018.
- JPY: ~+6% within days (Aug 2024 carry unwind).
- USD: +7–12% in a true dash for cash.
- Gold: ~+7% in Q4 2018, but **−10% to −12%** in liquidation phases (Mar 2020, Mar 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Drawdown frequency**
- Capital Group (1954–2024): declines of ≥5% about **twice per year**, ≥10% about **once every 18 months**, ≥20% about **once every six years**. An earlier version to Dec 2023 gave about once a year for 5% and once every 30 months for 10%, so the figures depend on end date and method. — [Capital Group](https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/planning/market-fluctuations/past-market-declines.html); [Capital Group (participant page)](https://www.capitalgroup.com/retirement/participant/basics/volatile-market/market-declines.html)
- Guggenheim, since 1946: **84** declines of 5–10% (more than one a year), **29** of 10–20% (about every 2.5 years), **9** of 20–40% (about every 8.5 years). — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2022/01/25/long-term-investors-shouldnt-worry-too-much-about-stocks-being-10percent-off-their-highs.html)
- Nasdaq Dorsey Wright: a 10% intra-year decline in almost two-thirds of years; a 20% drawdown in about a quarter of years. — [Dorsey Wright](https://dorseywright.nasdaq.com/research/bigwire/2024/12/24/12-24-2024/tuesday-feature-navigating-drawdowns)

**VIX spikes and normalisation**
- 16 episodes of VIX ≥45 since 1987. Normalisation (VIX below its level 50 days earlier) typically took ~**2.2 months**; the 2025 episode took ~1.3 months, the fastest in the dataset. — [RBC Wealth Management](https://ca.rbcwealthmanagement.com/The-Harbour-Group/blog/4537188-Sharp-Drop-in-Volatility-Bodes-Well-for-the-Future)
- A Yahoo Finance analysis back to the early 1990s found the latest move from VIX >30 to <20 took **8 trading days**, and argued resolutions have been getting faster. — [Trend Lab](https://trendlabs.com/volatility-mean-reverts/)
- An unverified TradingView benchmark gives the median VIX>30 regime at ~8 trading days (75th percentile ~15, 90th ~25). Treat it with caution. — search summary

### Inferences
- For an hours-to-1-day system, the base rate of "tradable" cross-asset shock days is high. In 2024–2026 alone the sources document distinct episodes in:
  - Aug 2024, Apr 2025, Oct 2025;
  - late Jan, early Feb, early March and late March 2026;
  - Jun 2026, and the September–October 2026 rate/oil shocks.

  That is about **3–6 episodes a year** with VIX or rate moves big enough to matter. This is my estimate from the cases above, not a statistical count.
- **Implication for position management:** impulses are short. Several 2025–2026 shocks reversed within 1–3 sessions on a policy U-turn (9 Apr 2025, 12 Oct 2025, 11 Jun 2026). Gap and reversal risk on "short the source asset" legs is therefore large. Holding beyond one day exposes the trade to policy-reversal gaps.

### Gaps
- No rigorous, peer-reviewed dataset was found on **intraday duration** (hours) of risk-off impulses or on conditional haven moves by VIX bucket. Building one from intraday data (ES, ZN, 6J, GC, BTC) would be needed.
- The frequency of VIX >30 episodes (as opposed to ≥45) was not found from a primary source.

## 4. Historical case studies with dated, quantified moves

### Takeaway
Each major episode had a different "winning haven":
- **2015:** classic risk-off.
- **Feb 2018:** a volatility-product blow-up with little haven signal.
- **Q4 2018:** long Treasuries and gold worked.
- **Mar 2020:** only USD and T-bills worked during the liquidation week; gold, long Treasuries and BTC were sold.
- **2022:** only USD, energy and cash worked; stocks and bonds fell together.
- **Mar 2023:** the 2Y Treasury was the strongest haven move.
- **Aug 2024:** JPY was the trade; markets recovered within days.
- **Apr 2025:** CHF, EUR, gold and 2Y Treasuries worked; the USD and long Treasuries failed.
- **Oct 2025:** a crypto leverage cascade with a ~$19bn liquidation.
- **2026:** an oil/inflation and rate-hike regime with gold crashes (30 Jan, March, Q2), 10Y >5%, BTC around 50% below its peak, and AI-rotation days.

## 5. When the classic safe havens failed, and what worked instead

### Takeaway
Each classic haven has a documented failure mode:

| Haven | Failure mode | Episodes |
|---|---|---|
| Gold | Fails in **liquidation/deleveraging** phases and when **real yields and the USD rise sharply** | 2008, Mar 2020, 30 Jan 2026, Mar 2026, Q2 2026, Sep 2026 |
| Long Treasuries | Fail in **inflation regimes** and **US fiscal/policy-credibility shocks** | 2022, Apr 2025, 2026 |
| USD | Fails in **US-centred policy shocks** | Apr 2025 |
| BTC | Has **never** reliably acted as a haven | 2020–2026 |

What worked instead: in liquidity crunches, USD and T-bills; in US-policy shocks, **CHF, EUR, gold, Bunds and 2Y Treasuries**; in inflation shocks, **USD, energy and short-dated bills**; in carry unwinds, the **JPY**.

### Cited Findings
- **Gold sold in liquidity crunches:**
  - Mar 2020: ~−10% from 24 Feb to 16 Mar, from selling for liquidity, futures liquidation, producer hedging and margin calls; the same happened in 2008. — [ii.co.uk](https://ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/why-did-safe-haven-gold-fall-value-during-market-sell-ii512586)
  - Mar 2026: −12%, attributed by the WGC mainly to forced liquidity needs and leveraged unwinds, not fundamentals. — [Gulf News/WGC](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fretail%2Fwhat-triggered-golds-march-fall-and-why-april-looks-different-1.500504903)
- **Gold sold when the hawkish policy outlook and yields jumped:**
  - 30 Jan 2026 Warsh nomination: −11.4% (futures). — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/silver-gold-tumble-triggers-major-183300200.html)
  - 23 Sep 2026: gold −7.26% over a month as the 10Y crossed 5%. — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/gold-selloff-shows-why-5-treasury-yields-are-the-bigger-threat-200688251)
- **Long Treasuries failed:**
  - 2022: 10Y Treasuries −16.7%.
  - Apr 2025: 10Y and 30Y became positively correlated with equities while the 2Y kept working.
  - Robeco's verdict: "US Treasuries have gone from safe haven to stormy waters"; suggested alternatives are Bunds, defensive equities, gold, commodities and fiat currencies.
  - [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)
- **Mar 2020:** Treasuries sold off; dealers could not absorb the selling. — [BIS speech](https://www.bis.org/speeches/20201019-what-happened-what-have-we-learned-it-lessons-covid-19-stress-financial-system)

  - The USD depreciated strongly while Treasury yields rose, the opposite of the usual risk-off pattern. This prompted debate on whether USD-asset safe-haven properties are changing. This is consistent with gold replacing long Treasuries as the hedge. — [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/index%2ephp/digest/us-treasury-bonds-and-trade-policy-uncertainty)
  - Investors moved into the franc and gold. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_stock_market_crash)
  - The euro outperformed. — [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2025/10/what-to-do-when-safe-havens-turn-into-stormy-waters)

### Inferences
- **Rule of thumb for a regime-aware haven selector:**
  - If the shock is **growth or financial-stability** driven (SVB, Aug 2024, Q4 2018), go long the **front end** (2Y/Schatz), JPY and CHF; gold works over the following 1–3 weeks.
  - If it is **inflation or rates** driven (2022, Jun 2026, Sep–Oct 2026), long bonds and gold fail; go long USD and energy, short duration and long-duration equities.
  - If it is a **US-credibility** shock (Apr 2025), avoid the USD and the long end; use CHF, EUR, gold, Bunds and the 2Y.
  - If it is a **liquidity crunch** (Mar 2020, Mar 2026 in gold), hold only USD and cash at first, then buy gold and long bonds once the central bank backstop arrives.
- **For a EUR-based trader**, Bunds and Schatz plus CHF give haven exposure without USD-translation risk in US-centred shocks.

### Gaps
- No source quantified CHF moves (e.g. EURCHF or USDCHF) in specific episodes. Robeco names the CHF as a haven but gives no drawdown data.
- Behaviour of Bunds and Schatz in the 2026 oil and rate shock was not found.

## 6. Which rotations are most persistent and exploitable, and what the evidence says about profitability

### Takeaway
The best-documented edges:

| Edge | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-asset trend following / time-series momentum | Positively skewed; performs in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns since 1880 and in the worst equity and bond environments since 1960 | Strongest academic evidence for "riding" rotations |
| Carry-crash dynamics | VIX up → funding currencies (JPY/CHF) up, investment currencies down | Documented |
| Rebalancing flows | ~17 bp next-day equity effect | Documented |
| Pre-FOMC drift | ~33 bp in the 24h before FOMC vs ~1 bp otherwise | Documented |
| Intraday market momentum | First half-hour return predicts the last half-hour, stronger on volatile and macro-news days | Documented; useful for an hours-to-1-day horizon |
| Gold after shocks | Short-lived haven, ~15 trading days | Weak; not a durable trend |
| Classic business-cycle sector rotation | ~2.3%/yr even with perfect hindsight; disappears after realistic timing and costs | Weak |
| Crypto altseason rotation | Practitioner lore only | Not tested |

### Cited Findings
- **Time-series momentum (AQR, "A Century of Evidence", 67 markets since 1880):** performed well in **8 of the 10** largest crisis periods, defined as the biggest 60/40 drawdowns, with positive average returns every decade and low correlation to traditional assets. These are hypothetical backtests. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/time-series-momentum-aka-trend-following-the-historical-evidence/)
- **Hamill, Rattray & Van Hemert (Man/Oxford, 1960–2015):** momentum worked before and after 1985 (in both bond bear and bull markets). Returns are positively skewed, like a long straddle, and strongest in the worst equity and bond environments. Barring long equity or bond positions improves crisis performance but lowers average returns. — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/trend-following-equity-and-bond-crisis-alpha)
- **Carry crash risk:** negative skewness; VIX increases coincide with carry unwinds and losses. The VIX predicts higher returns for investment currencies and lower returns for funding currencies; carry losses reduce future crash risk but raise its price. — [BNP NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473); [preliminary draft](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-08/M-Brunnermeier.pdf)
- **Safe-haven FX:** CHF and JPY appreciation on US stock declines works over hours to days. — [Ranaldo & Söderlind](https://www.snb.ch/en/mmr/papers/id/working_paper_2007_17)
- **Gold's haven effect is short-lived:** ~15 trading days in the 2006 working paper. Later Baur work on 68 markets reports less than 10 days for USD investors (search summary via IBKR Campus). — [Baur & Lucey](https://www.tcd.ie/triss/assets/PDFs/iiis/iiisdp198.pdf)
- **Intraday market momentum (Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, JFE 2018; SPY 1993–2013):**
  - The first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return.
  - The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and **major macro-news days**, and holds in 10 other major ETFs.
  - The working paper's out-of-sample R² is up to 1.8% (2.7% when the 12th half-hour is added).
  - [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- **Rebalancing:** next-day equity returns about −17 bp when equities are overweight; front-running is profitable. — [Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/what-do-pensions-lose-from-rebalancing)
- **Pre-FOMC drift:** ~33 bp in the 24h before FOMC vs ~1 bp on other days (1994–2011); none in Treasuries or the USD. — [NY Fed](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2012/07/the-puzzling-pre-fomc-announcement-drift)
- **Business-cycle sector rotation:**
  - With perfect foresight of NBER cycles: about **+2.3%/yr** (1948–2007), which loses statistical significance with realistic timing errors and costs. — [CXO Advisory on Stangl, Jacobsen & Visaltanachoti](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/economic-indicators/perfect-sector-rotation)
  - Molchanov & Stangl (2023): modest at best and quickly diminished; cross-industry predictability no better than chance. — [Massey University](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/69590)
  - A 2020 study found some positive excess returns for signal-based (momentum, rates) rotation in Europe and the US. — search summary
- **VIX spikes mean-revert:** typical normalisation in ~2.2 months for VIX ≥45 (1.3 months in 2025). — [RBC](https://ca.rbcwealthmanagement.com/The-Harbour-Group/blog/4537188-Sharp-Drop-in-Volatility-Bodes-Well-for-the-Future)
  - Schaeffer's found big VIX moves unreliable as one-year predictors. — [Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/analysis/2015/11/02/why-big-vix-moves-are-unreliable-indicators)
- **Carry rebuilds:** BIS warned leveraged positions were being rebuilt after Aug 2024, so similar volatility could recur. — [globaltrading.net summary of BIS](https://globaltrading.net/?p=36090)

### Inferences
- **Most exploitable for the stated horizon (hours to 1 day)**, in my synthesis:
  1. **Front-end rates and JPY/CHF on growth or financial-stability shocks.** These are large, directional moves documented in SVB 2023 and Aug 2024, with fast mean reversion afterwards.
  2. **Shocks around macro data releases** (NFP, CPI, PMI) in the current inflation regime. Intraday momentum is stronger on news days, and 2026 has shown repeated same-direction moves across long-end yields, USD, gold, Nasdaq and BTC.
  3. **Liquidation cascades in crypto**, where overshoot and fast rebound are the norm.
  4. **Month-end rebalancing reversals.**
- **Weakest for this horizon:** "gold trend after risk-off" (the haven effect fades in ~2–3 weeks, and gold is sold in deleveraging phases) and business-cycle sector rotation.
- **Trend following** offers the best-evidenced crisis payoff, but at daily-to-monthly horizons. An intraday system could use daily trend and regime signals as filters and take intraday entries on detected flow impulses.

### Gaps
- No published, out-of-sample evidence was found on the profitability of **intraday cross-asset "flow-following"** (e.g. short NQ / long ZN / long 6J on risk-off detection) for retail-size accounts after costs.
- Post-2015 performance of the pre-FOMC drift and post-2018 performance of intraday momentum were not verified.
- No study on the profitability of systematically fading crypto liquidation cascades was found.

## 7. How gold's role changed 2022–2026, and how Bitcoin behaved in risk-off episodes 2020–2026

### Inferences
- **Gold's 2022–2025 rally** was underpinned by structurally higher official-sector demand, about double the 2010–2021 average. That gave gold a "US-credibility hedge" role, visible in April 2025.
- **By 2026, positioning had become the dominant short-term driver.** Gold became leveraged, ETF-, CTA- and retail-driven, so in risk-off or hawkish-rate events it now behaves partly like a **risk asset to be liquidated**. Silver behaves even more so.
- **For a money-flow system,** gold should be treated as a **conditional** haven:
  - Long gold on US-credibility or geopolitical shocks when real yields are not rising.
  - Avoid or short gold on hawkish-rate or USD-surge days and in deleveraging cascades.
- **Bitcoin** should be modelled as a **leveraged Nasdaq/liquidity proxy** that trades 24/7. It often moves first on weekend or overnight shocks and amplifies them, which makes it useful as a **detection signal** and as a **short leg** in risk-off. It is not a destination for flight-to-safety flows.
